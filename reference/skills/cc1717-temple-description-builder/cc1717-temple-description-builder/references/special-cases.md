# Special Cases

Temples that will produce wrong or awkward copy if run through the standard field set unchanged. Check here before writing.

Nothing in this file substitutes for research. The entries describe *how the template breaks*, not the facts themselves. Verify every date and figure against the source hierarchy as usual.

## Undedicated temples

Temples under construction or awaiting dedication. Cody Wyoming was the first of these in this catalog; Wichita Kansas, Rosario Argentina, and Coeur d'Alene Idaho will follow.

The standard field set assumes a finished, operating building. For an undedicated temple:

- **Add a Status row first.** State plainly that the temple is under construction, give the open house dates if announced, and give the scheduled dedication date. Everything downstream depends on the reader knowing this.
- **Temple Number does not exist yet.** The number among dedicated temples is fixed at dedication. Use the state or country ordinal instead ("the third temple built in Wyoming") and say the dedicated number will be set at dedication.
- **Delete First Temple President.** Not called until near dedication.
- **Delete Rededicated and Changes Over Time.** Nothing has changed yet.
- **Write Dedicated as scheduled, not completed**, or fold it into the Status row. Never phrase a future dedication in the past tense.
- **Do not name the presiding officer unless Tier 1 names them.** Tier 2 sometimes lists a presider before the Church announces one. A future assignment attributed to a named apostle is a bad thing to get wrong.
- **Temple district is provisional.** Tier 2 states plainly that the final determination comes at dedication. Do not ship a stake count.
- **Expect a thin Symbolism and Design section.** Interior details and local motifs are usually not published until media day, a few days before the open house opens. Leave the section short rather than padding it, and tell the user when the details are likely to surface.

Warn the user that the entry will need rewriting twice: once after the open house closes, to add attendance figures, and once after dedication, to convert tenses and add the temple number, presiding officer, and first temple president. That is a real cost worth naming, and it may change whether they publish now or wait.

## Demolished and renamed temples

Some temples in this catalog no longer physically exist as described. The original building was demolished and a new, differently designed building is under construction or already dedicated on the same site, usually under a new name. Old Provo (1972, demolished 2024, replaced by the Provo Utah Rock Canyon Temple) is the first of these in the catalog. Nauvoo is related but distinct: Nauvoo's replacement has already been built and dedicated, so its product page describes the standing building rather than the vanished one. A demolished-and-not-yet-replaced temple needs different handling:

- **Add a Status row first**, the same instinct as the undedicated-temple case but pointed the other way. State plainly that the building described no longer stands, give the closure and demolition dates, and name the replacement building if one has been announced or is under construction. A reader should not have to infer from context that the product depicts a building they cannot visit.
- **Title the temple with its operating years** if the name changed, e.g. "Provo Utah Temple (1972–2024)," so the product page is legible on its own without requiring the reader to already know the history.
- **Everything else describes the old building, not the new one.** Architecture, ordinance rooms, symbolism, all of it are about the structure the shirt depicts. Do not blend in specs for the replacement building; that produces a page that is accurate about neither building.
- **Expect the myth screen to matter more here than usual.** A demolished mid-century building accumulates retroactive symbolic readings the way old buildings generally do, and the people who could confirm or deny them (architects, construction staff) are increasingly hard to reach. Demand the same primary-source standard as any other symbolism claim; do not relax it because the building is gone and feels more like history than product copy.
- **Do not backfill the new building's status into this page.** If the replacement is under construction, that belongs in the Status row as one sentence, not as its own set of fields. If the user later wants a product for the new building once it is dedicated, that is a new temple entry with its own research, not an edit to this one.

## Kirtland Temple, Ohio

The largest divergence in the series. Kirtland is **not an operating temple** and the standard fields produce false implications.

- It is a historic site open to public tours at no charge, not a house of the Lord in current use. Ordinance room counts, sealing rooms, baptistries, temple president, and temple district do not apply. Delete those rows rather than filling them.
- It was owned by Community of Christ (formerly the Reorganized Church of Jesus Christ of Latter Day Saints) for roughly 140 years before ownership transferred to The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints on March 5, 2024, as part of a $192.5 million agreement that also covered Nauvoo properties, manuscripts, and artifacts. The building reopened for public tours on March 25, 2024. That is a genuinely interesting story and belongs in the copy, told neutrally: the sale was received with sorrow by many Community of Christ members and joy by many Latter-day Saints, and the brand's voice should not celebrate at anyone's expense.
- It is a National Historic Landmark.
- Dedication was in 1836 under Joseph Smith. "First temple" claims need care about phrasing; first completed in this dispensation is the usual framing.
- Temple Number does not apply in the way it does elsewhere. Either delete the row or explain the distinction.

Use a modified field set: Location, Dedicated, Architecture, Historical Significance, Ownership History, Changes Over Time, Trivia.

## Washington D.C. Temple, Maryland

The temple is named for Washington D.C. but physically sits in **Kensington, Maryland**. The product caption reads "WASHINGTON D.C., MARYLAND," which looks like an error to anyone who doesn't know. Address this directly in the Location row and turn it into the first interesting fact rather than letting it read as a typo.

The temple's appearance from the Capital Beltway is a genuine piece of regional lore worth researching for Trivia.

It has been closed for major renovation and rededicated, so the Rededicated row applies. Verify both dates and both officiators; the original dedication and the rededication are frequently conflated.

## Salt Lake Temple, Utah

Closed for a seismic and renovation project, so a large share of the entry is volatile:

- Renovation completion, open house, and rededication dates have all shifted more than once. Anything about the current project goes in the ledger's Volatile section without exception.
- The angel Moroni statue and its trumpet have a live status. The statue was removed by crane in May 2020 after the trumpet fell in the March 18, 2020 earthquake. Confirm current status before writing about it in the present tense.
- Room counts reflect the post-renovation configuration and may not match older sources.
- See `known-myths.md`. This temple has more folklore attached to it than the rest of the series combined.

## Recently dedicated temples

Saratoga Springs, Taylorsville, and similar. The standard field set assumes decades of history. Recent temples have little, and padding produces obvious filler.

Lean instead on what recent temples document unusually well:

- **Design motifs.** Modern temples are explicitly designed around local symbolism: a native plant in the art glass, a pattern drawn from regional history, a palette chosen to reference the landscape. The Church publishes this. It is the single best material for a recent temple and it ties directly to the drawing on the shirt.
- **Open house attendance figures**, which Newsroom publishes.
- **The temple district** and what it changed for local members, including travel distances before and after.
- **Announcement context**: which general conference, by whom, and what else was announced alongside it.

A shorter, denser entry is better than a padded one. Match depth across the batch by trimming the long entries rather than inflating the short ones.

## General guidance for temples not listed here

Ask four questions before applying the standard field set:

1. **Is it dedicated?** If not, see Undedicated temples above.
2. **Is it an operating temple?** Historic sites, visitors' centers, and buildings under renovation need field changes.
3. **Does the name match the location?** Several temples are named for a metropolitan area rather than the municipality they sit in. Where the product caption could confuse a buyer, explain it.
4. **Has it been rededicated?** Older temples usually have been, often more than once, and the rededication is frequently reported as the dedication.

When a temple turns out to need special handling, add an entry here.
