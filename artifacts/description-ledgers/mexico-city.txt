SOURCE LEDGER - Mexico City Mexico Temple

VERIFIED
[T1] Announced April 3, 1976 after Spencer W. Kimball met Mexican Church leaders in March 1976; groundbreaking Nov 25, 1979 (Elder Boyd K. Packer); dedicated Dec 2-4, 1983 by Gordon B. Hinckley in nine sessions, his second temple dedication in North America; rededicated Nov 16, 2008 by Thomas S. Monson in two sessions after ~19 months of work; rededicated again Sept 13, 2015 by Henry B. Eyring in three sessions - thechurchnews.com almanac
[T1] Open house Nov 9-19, 1983, more than 120,000 visitors, up to 20,000 in a day; the visitor who returned with 29 friends and asked to bring 100 more; members previously traveled ~1,500 miles to the Mesa Arizona Temple; Mexico became the first country outside the U.S. with 100 stakes on July 25, 1989 - thechurchnews.com almanac
[T1] Building size 35,551 sq ft; height 151 ft not including the 15.5-ft Moroni; white cast stone / precast concrete exterior inspired by ancient Mesoamerican architecture; basket weave band encircling the exterior; Mesoamerican motifs in celestial room carpet carving and art glass; oak and cast bronze doors installed 2015; carved waterfall columns in the millwork; marble quarried in Spain and fabricated in China; district serves 384,000+ members across Mexico City and 11 states - newsroom.churchofjesuschrist.org fact sheet, thechurchnews.com (Sept 17, 2015)
[T1] 2007-2008 project: closed March 2007, seismic enhancements, foundation work to fortify the structure, replacement of the precast exterior panels, Moroni refurbishment, new landscaping, remodel of baptistry, lobby, corridors, sealing and ordinance rooms - newsroom.churchofjesuschrist.org, thechurchnews.com
[T1] Renovation architect Valentiner Crane Brunjes Onyon, Salt Lake City - newsroom.churchofjesuschrist.org fact sheet
[T2] 26th dedicated temple; first in Mexico; original architect Emil B. Fetzer; four instruction rooms, eleven sealing rooms, one baptistry; elevation 7,305 ft; Colonia San Juan de Aragon, near Bosque de Aragon; building had dropped at least eight feet by its 25th anniversary as Mexico City subsides - churchofjesuschristtemples.org

CONFLICTS RESOLVED
Floor area - T2 says 116,642 sq ft, T1 newsroom fact sheet and Church News both say 35,551, and the Church News renovation coverage repeats 35,551 when describing the foundation work. Tier 1 wins outright; 35,551 shipped. The T2 figure most likely covers the whole complex rather than the temple building.
Site size - T2 says 7 acres, T1 fact sheet says 1.82 acres. Same split as the floor area and unresolved as to what each measures, so no acreage shipped at all.
Height - T1 gives 151 ft excluding a 15.5-ft statue, T2 gives 166 ft 6 in. These reconcile exactly (151 + 15.5 = 166.5). Shipped in the T1 form, which makes the distinction explicit.
Architect - T2 credits Emil B. Fetzer, T1 credits Valentiner Crane Brunjes Onyon. Different jobs: Fetzer designed the 1983 original, VCBO handled the 2007-2008 renovation. Both shipped, each labeled.

VOLATILE (re-check before reprint)
"Eight feet" of subsidence - measured to the 25th anniversary in 2008; the ground has kept sinking since
District membership of 384,000+ - grows continuously
"More than two dozen temples in Mexico" - Church News counted 27 in Oct 2025; deliberately written loosely for that reason
Room counts - the 2015 remodel post-dates some published figures

OMITTED
"Largest temple outside the United States" - T2 asserts it, but it cannot stand next to the Tier 1 floor area of 35,551 sq ft, and no Tier 1 source repeats it. Dropped rather than shipped with a hedge.
"Received an international architectural award" - T2 only, no award named, no corroboration
First temple president - not published on any tiered source consulted
