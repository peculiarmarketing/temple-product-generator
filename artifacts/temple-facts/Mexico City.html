Mexico City Mexico Temple

Location
Mexico City, Mexico. The temple stands in the Colonia San Juan de Aragón district of northeastern Mexico City, near the Bosque de Aragón, at an elevation of about 7,305 feet.

Temple Number
The 26th dedicated temple, and the first built in Mexico.

Announced
April 3, 1976, following President Spencer W. Kimball's meeting with Mexican Church leaders the month before.

Groundbreaking
November 25, 1979. Elder Boyd K. Packer of the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles presided.

Dedicated
December 2 to 4, 1983, by President Gordon B. Hinckley in nine sessions. It was his second temple dedication in North America.

Rededicated
November 16, 2008, by President Thomas S. Monson in two sessions, after a renovation of about 19 months. Rededicated again September 13, 2015, by President Henry B. Eyring in three sessions, following an interior remodel and exterior cleaning.

Architecture
Originally designed by Church architect Emil B. Fetzer; the 2007 to 2008 renovation was handled by Valentiner Crane Brunjes Onyon of Salt Lake City. The exterior is white cast stone worked into adaptations of ancient Mesoamerican forms: a rectangular base, a basket weave band, and stepped levels that diminish as they rise. About 35,551 square feet, standing 151 feet to the top of the building, with a 15.5 foot angel Moroni statue above that.

Ordinance Rooms
Four instruction rooms, eleven sealing rooms, and one baptistry.

Construction Story

  • Before the temple opened, members in Mexico traveled roughly 1,500 miles to the Mesa Arizona Temple.
  • The public open house ran November 9 through November 19, 1983 and drew more than 120,000 visitors, with as many as 20,000 coming on a single day.
  • One visitor returned with 29 friends and asked permission to bring a hundred more.
  • Marble used in the building was quarried in Spain and fabricated in China.

Symbolism & Design

  • A basket weave pattern encircles the exterior, one of several motifs drawn from ancient Mesoamerican building traditions rather than from the Church's standard temple vocabulary.
  • The Mesoamerican reference carries inside as well, in carved carpet in the celestial room and in the art glass.
  • Millwork in the building includes carved columns worked with a waterfall design. The oak and cast bronze doors were installed in 2015.

Changes Over Time

  • Mexico City's ground is subsiding, and the temple has gone down with it. By the temple's 25th anniversary the building had dropped at least eight feet in elevation.
  • The temple closed in March 2007 for about 19 months. The work included seismic strengthening, foundation work to fortify the structure, replacement of the precast concrete exterior panels with panels matching the original, refurbishment of the angel Moroni statue, new landscaping, and remodeling of the baptistry, lobby, corridors, sealing rooms, and ordinance rooms.
  • A further interior remodel and exterior cleaning preceded the 2015 rededication.

Trivia

  • Five and a half years after the dedication, on July 25, 1989, Mexico became the first country outside the United States with 100 stakes.
  • The temple district serves more than 384,000 members across Mexico City and eleven surrounding states.
  • It was the first of what has grown to more than two dozen temples in Mexico.
