Billings Montana Temple

Location
Billings, Montana. A 10-acre site at 3100 Rim Point Drive, on the rimrocks on the west side of the city, above the Yellowstone Valley.

Temple Number
The 66th dedicated temple in operation, and the first built in Montana.

Announced
August 16, 1996, by Gordon B. Hinckley.

Groundbreaking
March 28, 1998, presided over by Hugh W. Pinnock of the Seventy, then president of the North America Central Area.

Dedicated
November 20 and 21, 1999, by Gordon B. Hinckley, in eight sessions attended by more than 12,000 members.

Architecture
Wyoming white dolomite precast concrete, 33,800 square feet, with a single central spire carrying the angel Moroni.

Ordinance Rooms
Two instruction rooms, arranged for two-stage progressive sessions, three sealing rooms, and one baptistry.

Construction Story

  • A spring snowstorm arrived for the groundbreaking and covered the site. More than 4,800 people came from across the United States and Canada and stood in it anyway.
  • The public open house ran October 8 to October 23, 1999, and drew more than 68,450 visitors.

Symbolism & Design

  • A clear skylight over the main foyer was placed so that patrons inside can look up and see the angel Moroni on the spire above them.
  • A stained glass bay window fills the west end of the building, the side facing out over the valley.
  • The building is a study in fitting a full temple into a small footprint. Laundry, brides' room, chapel and clothing rental desk are all worked into 33,800 square feet.

Trivia

  • Before it was built, the nearest house of the Lord was the Idaho Falls Idaho Temple. Members from Billings counted on six hours of driving in summer and eight or more in winter.
  • The grounds were planted with more than 250 trees and 4,500 shrubs.
  • Its district reached beyond one state, serving Latter-day Saints across Montana and into Wyoming.

Temple facts verified as of September 16, 2026.
