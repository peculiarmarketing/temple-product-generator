Red Cliffs Utah Temple

Location
St. George, Utah, on 15.31 acres in the Little Valley area on the city's east side. The temple is named for the red sandstone cliffs of the region rather than for a town, which is why the caption reads St. George: this is the second temple in that city.

Temple Number
The 189th dedicated temple, and the fourth in southern Utah.

Announced
October 7, 2018, by President Russell M. Nelson, in general conference.

Groundbreaking
November 7, 2020. Elder Jeffrey R. Holland presided, before roughly seventy invited guests, the ceremony having been kept small under local COVID-19 restrictions.

Dedicated
March 24, 2024, by President Henry B. Eyring of the First Presidency, in two sessions.

Architecture
Three stories in coral and beige precast concrete panels cast with cottonwood leaf motifs, under a multilevel spire with arched windows. 96,277 square feet, rising 217 feet to the top of the spire and 230 feet to the top of the angel Moroni statue.

Ordinance Rooms
Four instruction rooms, six sealing rooms, one baptistry.

First Temple President
J. Kevin Ence, with matron Debbie Ence.

Construction Story

  • Five and a half years passed between the announcement and the dedication.
  • The public open house ran February 1 through March 2, 2024, excluding Sundays.

Symbolism & Design

  • The exterior takes its motifs from the cottonwood trees, mountains and canyons of southern Utah, with stylized cottonwood leaves worked into the precast panels.
  • The art glass carries local plants in coral, purple, green and gold: succulents, Indian paintbrush, and the dwarf bearclaw poppy, a rare species that grows nowhere in the world but this corner of Utah. Pomegranates appear as well.
  • Interior stonework is laid in horizontal bands that echo the strata in the cliffs and canyons nearby.
  • Palm trees line the entrance walk.

Trivia

  • President Eyring's great-grandfather, Henry Eyring, was one of the pioneers who settled the St. George area and served as a recorder in the St. George Utah Temple.
  • For five weeks after its dedication, until the Urdaneta Philippines Temple was dedicated in late April 2024, St. George held both the oldest and the newest operating temples in the Church.

Temple facts verified as of August 23, 2026.
