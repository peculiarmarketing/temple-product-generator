Monticello Utah Temple

Location
Monticello, Utah, in San Juan County in the southeast corner of the state.

Temple Number
The 53rd dedicated temple, and the eleventh built in Utah.

Announced
October 4, 1997, as the first of the smaller temples President Gordon B. Hinckley had introduced earlier that year.

Groundbreaking
November 17, 1997. Elder Ben B. Banks presided.

Dedicated
July 26 to 27, 1998, by President Gordon B. Hinckley.

Rededicated
November 17, 2002, by President Gordon B. Hinckley, after the building was enlarged.

Architecture
Turkish off white marble. 11,225 square feet following the 2002 expansion, up from 7,000 as originally built, rising 66 feet to a single attached spire with an angel Moroni statue.

Ordinance Rooms
Two instruction rooms, two sealing rooms, one baptistry.

Construction Story

  • This was the first of the small temples, a design President Gordon B. Hinckley announced in 1997 to put temples within reach of members far from the existing ones.
  • The plan left out the laundry, the cafeteria and other space not needed for ordinances. That made the building far smaller and much faster to put up.
  • It went from groundbreaking to dedication in just over eight months, faster than any temple built before it.
  • As first completed, at 7,000 square feet, it had less than half the floor space of an ordinary meetinghouse.

Symbolism & Design

  • The exterior is clad in Turkish off white marble.
  • The first angel Moroni statue placed here was white fiberglass and stood six feet tall, against the twelve foot gold leafed figures used on earlier temples.

Changes Over Time

  • The temple was enlarged and remodeled in 2002, growing from 7,000 to 11,225 square feet, with a second instruction room and a second sealing room added. Temple president Lisle G. Adams explained the reason plainly: "The facilities we had were just not adequate to take care of the work that was being done."
  • President Hinckley rededicated the enlarged building on November 17, 2002, having dedicated the original four years earlier.
  • The white statue was later replaced with a gold leafed one.

Trivia

  • It was the first of the small temples, a design the Church has used many times since.
  • The temple was dedicated less than ten months after it was announced.

Temple facts verified as of August 20, 2026.
