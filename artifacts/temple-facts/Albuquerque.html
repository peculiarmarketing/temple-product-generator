Albuquerque New Mexico Temple

Location
Albuquerque, New Mexico. An 8.5-acre site at 10301 San Francisco Road NE, on the northeast heights of the city at an elevation of 5,729 feet, with the Sandia Mountains rising directly to the east.

Temple Number
The 73rd dedicated temple in operation, and the first built in New Mexico.

Announced
April 4, 1997, by Gordon B. Hinckley.

Groundbreaking
June 20, 1998, presided over by Lynn A. Mickelsen of the Seventy, then president of the North America Southwest Area.

Dedicated
March 5, 2000, by Gordon B. Hinckley, in four sessions attended by more than 13,500 Latter-day Saints.

Architecture
Fanning Bard Tatum Architects of Albuquerque. Desert Rose precast concrete with Texas Pearl granite at the base, 34,245 square feet, with a single attached end spire carrying the angel Moroni.

Ordinance Rooms
Two instruction rooms, three sealing rooms, and one baptistry.

Construction Story

  • The public open house ran February 17 to February 26, 2000, nine days, and drew roughly 70,000 visitors.
  • The mountains the temple was sited against turned out to have an opinion about the front door. The original hinged entrance doors were replaced with glass sliding doors because of the winds coming off the Sandias.

Symbolism & Design

  • The exterior was chosen to answer the landscape rather than stand apart from it. The Desert Rose concrete and Texas Pearl granite read pink in the evening light, which is exactly what the Sandia range does at sunset, a colour the mountains are named for.
  • Church News described the building as the first of its design in the Church, and framed the temple as a meeting point for a region where three cultures come together: American Indian, Hispanic and Anglo.

Changes Over Time

  • A 2019 renovation converted the original kitchen and cafeteria into office space, a change made across many temples of this era as patron meal service was discontinued.

Trivia

  • The temple stands at 5,729 feet above sea level, on the high desert bench that runs up toward the mountains from the Rio Grande.
  • It arrived in a crowded season. The Church's May 2000 conference report covered seven temple dedications in a single article, Albuquerque among them.
