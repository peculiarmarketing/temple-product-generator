Provo Utah Rock Canyon Temple

Status
Under construction, and not yet dedicated. This building replaces the Provo Utah Temple of 1972, which stood on the same ground and closed February 24, 2024. Exterior cladding and windows are going in and the grounds are being landscaped. No open house or dedication dates have been announced.

Location
Provo, Utah. A 15.4-acre site on the city's east bench at the mouth of Rock Canyon, which is where the new name comes from.

Temple Number
Not yet fixed. The number is set at dedication.

Architecture
Bianco Sardo granite quarried in Sardinia, 163,000 square feet, 265 feet 6 inches to the top of a single attached central spire. Built to current seismic codes, with reconfigured rooms and new electrical, heating and plumbing systems.

Ordinance Rooms
Six instruction rooms, eight sealing rooms, and two baptistries.

Construction Story

  • It did not go back on the same footprint. The replacement was built west of the original foundation, on a flatter part of the slope, making it the third reconstructed temple to be moved off its original footprint.
  • The Church announced the change of name with the reconstruction, so the building that reopens will carry a different name from the one that closed.

Symbolism & Design

  • The most visible break with its predecessor is what is missing. The Rock Canyon design carries no angel Moroni, where the 1972 building had one above its single gold spire.
  • Interior details, art glass and palette have not been published. For a temple at this stage those normally arrive at media day, shortly before an open house opens.

Temple facts verified as of September 16, 2026.
