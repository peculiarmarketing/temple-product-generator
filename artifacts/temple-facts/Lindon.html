Lindon Utah Temple

Location
Lindon, Utah, in Utah County, on the east side of the valley below the Wasatch Range.

Temple Number
The 216th dedicated temple. It is the 25th in Utah and the seventh in Utah County.

Announced
October 4, 2020, by President Russell M. Nelson, at general conference.

Groundbreaking
April 23, 2022. Elder Kevin W. Pearson, president of the Utah Area, presided.

Dedicated
May 3, 2026, by President Henry B. Eyring, First Counselor in the First Presidency.

Architecture
Site and landscape design by MHTN Architects of Salt Lake City. The exterior is Natural White Moncini stone, quarried and fabricated in Italy by Campolonghi Italia. 83,140 square feet, rising 224 feet, with two attached end spires.

Ordinance Rooms
Four instruction rooms, four sealing rooms, two baptistries.

Construction Story

  • Five and a half years passed between the announcement and the dedication, with construction beginning in April 2022.
  • The exterior stone was quarried and cut in Italy before being shipped to the site.
  • The public open house ran March 12 through April 11, 2026, and drew more than 280,000 visitors.

Symbolism & Design

  • The linden tree and its flower, which give the city its name, run through the whole building. They appear in the art glass, the carved millwork, the interior stonework, the railings and the decorative paint.
  • Sheaves of wheat gathered in a harvest basket appear as a second motif, referring to Lindon's agricultural past.
  • The exterior is faced in Natural White Moncini stone, quarried and fabricated by Campolonghi Italia in Italy and shipped to Utah.

Trivia

  • More than 280,000 people toured the temple during its open house, held over about four weeks in the spring of 2026.
  • The building has two baptistries and four sealing rooms across 83,140 square feet.

Temple facts verified as of August 20, 2026.
