Vernal Utah Temple

Location
Vernal, Utah, on 1.6 acres two blocks south of Main Street in the Ashley Valley.

Temple Number
The 51st dedicated temple.

Announced
February 13, 1994, when local leaders told members the historic Uintah Stake Tabernacle would become a house of the Lord.

Groundbreaking
May 13, 1995. President Gordon B. Hinckley presided.

Dedicated
November 2 to 4, 1997, by President Gordon B. Hinckley, in eleven sessions. About 3,528 people attended the first session.

Architecture
FFKR Architects of Salt Lake City, with Roger Jackson as chief architect, working inside the brick shell of the 1907 tabernacle. Face brick, one of the few temples without a light colored exterior. 38,771 square feet, rising 98 feet.

Ordinance Rooms
Two instruction rooms in a two stage progressive arrangement, three sealing rooms, one baptistry.

First Temple President
Alva C. Snow.

Construction Story

  • This was the first temple built from an existing building. The Uintah Stake Tabernacle, dedicated August 24, 1907, by President Joseph F. Smith, had fallen into serious disrepair and had not hosted a stake conference since 1983.
  • The interior was removed entirely. As the project put it, they kept the outside and built a new building inside.
  • About 1,500 crumbling bricks in the outer wall had to be replaced, and matching brick no longer existed. The answer was the Reader Home, a turn of the century house in Vernal whose brick had come from the same kiln as the tabernacle's, the Abner N. Swain Brickworks. Crews spent two months taking roughly 16,000 bricks out of the house by hand.
  • The public open house ran October 11 through 25, 1997, and drew about 118,700 visitors.

Symbolism & Design

  • A historic plaque describes the tabernacle's form as a simplified and almost civic variant of the Georgian New England church. The temple kept that form deliberately.
  • The west tower was rebuilt to the original specifications, and a matching tower was raised at the east end, where the tabernacle never had one.
  • The angel Moroni statue was placed on that new east tower on September 16, 1996. It went up painted gold; four months later the decision was made to give it the traditional gold leaf finish instead.
  • The building carries two dates, 1907 and 1997, the years it was dedicated as a tabernacle and then as a temple.
  • The celestial room armchairs are replicas, in horsehair fabric, of the chairs used by the First Presidency and the Quorum of the Twelve in the Salt Lake Temple.

Trivia

  • It stood as the only temple made from an existing tabernacle for nearly two decades, until the Provo City Center Temple was dedicated in 2016.
  • President Gordon B. Hinckley both broke ground for the temple and dedicated it, two and a half years apart.
