Deseret Peak Utah Temple

Location
Tooele, Utah. The temple is named for Deseret Peak, the fourth most prominent summit in the state, rather than for the city it stands in. It sits in Tooele Valley about 30 miles west of Salt Lake City.

Temple Number
The 200th dedicated temple. It is the 23rd in Utah and the first in Tooele County.

Announced
April 7, 2019, by President Russell M. Nelson, at general conference.

Groundbreaking
May 15, 2021. Elder Brook P. Hales of the Quorum of the Seventy presided.

Dedicated
November 10, 2024, by President Russell M. Nelson.

Architecture
FFKR Architects. Glass fiber reinforced concrete cast with dolomite aggregate quarried from the Tooele Valley. 71,998 square feet, rising 182 feet 8 inches to a single attached central tower.

Ordinance Rooms
Four instruction rooms, four sealing rooms, one baptistry.

Construction Story

  • Five and a half years passed between the announcement and the dedication, with construction beginning in May 2021.
  • The public open house ran September 26 through October 19, 2024, and drew more than 130,000 visitors.
  • President Nelson was 100 years old when he dedicated the building.

Symbolism & Design

  • The exterior concrete carries dolomite aggregate quarried from the Tooele Valley itself, so the building is faced in part with stone from the ground it stands on.
  • Native regional plants recur throughout: lupine, cliff rose, salt grass and valley grasses appear in the exterior stone, millwork, rugs, decorative paint, font railings and art glass.
  • The design draws on the historic tabernacles built in the surrounding valley.

Trivia

  • This was the Church's 200th dedicated temple. It took until the year 2000 to dedicate the first 100, and 24 years to dedicate the second 100.
  • Deseret Peak itself rises in the Stansbury Mountains west of the temple and is the fourth most prominent peak in Utah.
