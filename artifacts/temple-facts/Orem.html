Orem Utah Temple

Location
Orem, Utah, on 15.39 acres at 1451 South 1100 West, on the valley floor west of the city toward Utah Lake.

Temple Number
The 188th dedicated temple. It is the 19th in Utah and the sixth in Utah County.

Announced
October 5, 2019, by President Russell M. Nelson, in general conference.

Groundbreaking
September 5, 2020. Elder Craig C. Christensen, Utah Area President, presided.

Dedicated
January 21, 2024, by Elder D. Todd Christofferson of the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles, in two sessions.

Architecture
Three stories clad in Pedras Salgadas granite from Portugal, with bronze colored doors and taupe window frames. 71,998 square feet, rising 218 feet to the top of the spire.

Ordinance Rooms
Four instruction rooms seating fifty each, four sealing rooms, one baptistry.

First Temple President
Kenneth Lee Sorber, with matron Kristy Nelson Sorber.

Construction Story

  • Four years and three months passed between the announcement and the dedication, with the groundbreaking coming less than a year after the announcement.
  • The public open house ran October 27 through December 16, 2023, excluding Sundays and Thanksgiving.

Symbolism & Design

  • The cherry tree is the governing motif, and the building uses the whole tree rather than just the flower: blossoms, leaves, fruit and branches recur in the art glass, the wood paneling, the metal handrails, the carpets and the light fixtures.
  • The choice points at what Orem used to be. Fruit growing built the town, and after residents named the settlement Orem in 1919, trainloads of fruit moved out on Walter Orem's railroad, earning the area the nickname Garden City of Utah.
  • Floors are Crema Marfil marble quarried in Turkey and fabricated in California. Marble wainscots and wood columns run through the building.

Trivia

  • It is the first temple in Orem, in a county that already had five.
  • At 218 feet it is taller than the Ogden Utah Temple, on a building barely two thirds the floor area.

Temple facts verified as of August 23, 2026.
