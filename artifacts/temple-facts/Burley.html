Burley Idaho Temple

Location
Burley, Idaho. A 10.12-acre site at 40 South 150 East, in the Magic Valley farming country of south central Idaho.

Temple Number
The 212th dedicated temple in operation, and the seventh dedicated in Idaho.

Announced
April 4, 2021, by Russell M. Nelson at the close of the 191st Annual General Conference.

Groundbreaking
June 4, 2022, presided over by Brent H. Nielson of the Presidency of the Seventy.

Dedicated
January 11, 2026, by Dallin H. Oaks. It was the first temple he dedicated as President of the Church, and the first dedication of 2026.

Architecture
Naylor Wentworth Lund Architects. A two-story steel frame clad in Bianco Sardo granite, fabricated in Millcreek, Utah. 45,300 square feet, 172 feet to the top of a single attached end spire.

Ordinance Rooms
Four instruction rooms, three sealing rooms, and one baptistry.

Construction Story

  • The ground it stands on was given. Leroy Funk and his siblings donated farmland their family had worked for decades. Funk put the credit elsewhere: "It is here because of the faithful ones who came before."
  • Latter-day Saints have been in the Burley area since 1879. The first branch, fourteen members, was organized in a log cabin in May 1880.
  • The public open house ran November 6 to November 22, 2025, through a stretch of unseasonably warm and sunny weather that had the tour coordinators joking about handing out sunscreen in November.

Symbolism & Design

  • The art glass is drawn from the crop the valley is known for. Its patterns are the blossoms and leaves of the potato plant, with the sugar beet fields of the Magic Valley informing the wider design.
  • The color palette was taken from the farmland and the open sky above it: blue, gold, amber, cream, orange, peach and green.
  • Interior stonework is Botticino Classico marble with Jerusalem Gold and Verde Guatemala accents, and the artwork inside was commissioned around the local landscape and the early Latter-day Saints of Burley.

Trivia

  • President Oaks chose Burley for his first dedication as President of the Church because of where he grew up. He lived in nearby Twin Falls as a boy for about five years, and said the place was "close to my heart."
  • The drive to the temple went from about 45 minutes to about five. Before Burley, members here attended the Twin Falls Idaho Temple.
  • Susan Young, Twin Falls temple matron from 2016 to 2019, remembered Burley youth arriving for baptisms at 3:30 in the morning, week after week. The baptistry would be full of young people sitting in white, and quiet enough to hear a pin drop.
  • The open house was run to be reachable: 143 tours in Spanish, nine in American Sign Language, and one tactile tour for visitors who needed it.

Temple facts verified as of September 16, 2026.
