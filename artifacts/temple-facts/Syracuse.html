Syracuse Utah Temple

Location
Syracuse, Utah. The temple stands on a 12.268-acre site at 1098 South 2500 West, surrounded by farmland between the Great Salt Lake and Interstate 15, about 30 miles northwest of Salt Lake City, at an elevation of 4,275 feet.

Temple Number
The Church's 206th dedicated temple. At its dedication it was the 24th temple dedicated in Utah and the third built in Davis County, following the Bountiful Utah Temple (1995) and the Layton Utah Temple (2024).

Announced
April 5, 2020, by President Russell M. Nelson, in the Sunday afternoon session of a general conference broadcast from Temple Square without a congregation because of the COVID-19 pandemic. It was one of eight temples announced that day, a list that also included Dubai, United Arab Emirates, and Shanghai, People's Republic of China.

Groundbreaking
June 12, 2021. Elder Kevin R. Duncan, General Authority Seventy, presided at the ceremony and offered the site dedicatory prayer.

Dedicated
June 8, 2025, by President Russell M. Nelson, in a single session at 4 p.m., accompanied by Elder Quentin L. Cook of the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles. The service was broadcast to meetinghouses throughout the temple district.

Architecture
A three-story steel-frame structure clad in white Moncini granite, with 90,526 square feet of floor area rising 219 feet 8 inches to the top of its single attached end spire.

Ordinance Rooms
Four instruction rooms, four sealing rooms, and two baptistries.

First Temple President
Val Dean Steel, with Luann Kay Flint Steel as matron. Their calls were announced in November 2024, and they began serving at the temple's dedication.

Construction Story

  • The site was announced on August 28, 2020: a 12-acre field at the intersection of 2500 West and 1025 South, still surrounded by working farms. The official exterior rendering followed on February 16, 2021, showing a three-story building of roughly 89,000 square feet.
  • Elder Kevin R. Duncan, who presided at the groundbreaking, grew up on this ground, and his great-grandfather was the first bishop of Syracuse. "I am very familiar with these fields where we stand, where we are gathered today," he said at the ceremony. "From this temple site, I can see the barn roof of my childhood home."
  • The temple was announced in part to ease demand on the Ogden Utah Temple, which was then serving members of 63 stakes. At the announcement, Utah was home to 2.1 million Church members, roughly two-thirds of the state's population.
  • Materials crossed the Atlantic in both directions: the exterior granite was fabricated by Campolonghi of Montignoso, Italy, and installed by IMS Masonry of Lindon, Utah, while the art glass was fabricated by Progetto Arte Poli of Verona, Italy, and installed by Steel Encounters of Salt Lake City.
  • The open house began with a media day on May 7, 2025, followed by tours for invited guests, then a public open house from May 10 to May 31, 2025, excluding Sundays.

Symbolism & Design

  • Design motifs feature the flora of the Great Salt Lake and its nearby wetlands, including the snowball sand-verbena, desert paintbrush, common cattail, and reeds, rendered in shades of blues, greens, golds, yellows, and reds drawn from the area's indigenous plants.
  • The transitioning greens, blues, and yellows in the art glass represent reflections of the lake, a nod to Syracuse's identity as the "Gateway to Antelope Island." The glass features faceted crystal and was designed by Architectural Nexus with assistance from Holdman Studios.
  • Millwork and stain-grade doors are plain sawn sapele, the celestial room carpet is 100 percent New Zealand wool, and the crystal chandeliers were fabricated by Schonbek of Plattsburgh, New York.

Trivia

  • It was the first temple in the Church dedicated with two baptismal fonts. "The reason for that is the high concentration of members and especially high concentration of youth," Elder Kevin R. Duncan explained during the open house. "Now they can come more frequently."
  • Its dedication marked the 200th temple to open its doors during President Russell M. Nelson's 100-year life, and it was the fifth temple he personally dedicated: Sapporo Japan as President of the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles, then Concepción Chile, Rome Italy, Deseret Peak Utah, and Syracuse as Church President.
  • The Church's first ward in Syracuse was created on December 1, 1895, nearly 130 years before the city received a temple. The temple's district comprises 16 northern Davis County stakes in Syracuse, West Point, Clearfield, Clinton, and Sunset.
