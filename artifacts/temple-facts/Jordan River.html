Jordan River Utah Temple

Location
South Jordan, Utah. The temple takes its name from the Jordan River, which runs through the valley below it, rather than from the city it stands in. The site was donated to the Church.

Temple Number
The 20th dedicated temple.

Announced
February 3, 1978, by President Spencer W. Kimball.

Groundbreaking
June 9, 1979. President Spencer W. Kimball presided, before a crowd of about 10,000.

Dedicated
November 16 to 20, 1981, by President Marion G. Romney of the First Presidency, in 15 sessions.

Rededicated
May 20, 2018, by President Henry B. Eyring, following a two year renovation.

Architecture
Cast stone set with white marble chips. The tower looks like the same material but is built of cemlite, a fiberglass product chosen to reduce weight. 148,236 square feet, rising 219 feet to a single attached central spire with an angel Moroni statue.

Ordinance Rooms
Six instruction rooms, sixteen sealing rooms, one baptistry.

Construction Story

  • This was the first temple of the modern era built entirely with donated funds. By the May 1979 deadline, members of the district had raised 110 percent of the amount needed, and had done it ahead of schedule.
  • Rather than turn the first earth with a ceremonial shovel, President Kimball put on a hard hat, climbed onto a front end loader and scooped it himself, in keeping with his counsel to lengthen our stride.
  • The ground the temple stands on was given to the Church rather than purchased.

Symbolism & Design

  • The exterior is cast stone studded with white marble chips, which catches light across the face of the building.
  • The tower had to be lighter than it looks. It is built of cemlite, a fiberglass material, so that the structure below could carry it.

Changes Over Time

  • The temple closed in 2016 for a full renovation, which brought seismic upgrades, modernized mechanical and electrical systems and a redesigned interior.
  • President Henry B. Eyring rededicated the building on May 20, 2018.

Trivia

  • The building covers 148,236 square feet and holds sixteen sealing rooms.
  • About 10,000 people attended the groundbreaking in 1979.

Temple facts verified as of August 20, 2026.
