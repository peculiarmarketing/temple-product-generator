Draper Utah Temple

Location
Draper, Utah, on a hillside at the south end of the Salt Lake Valley, below Corner Canyon.

Temple Number
The 129th dedicated temple.

Announced
October 2, 2004, by President Gordon B. Hinckley, in the Saturday morning session of general conference.

Groundbreaking
August 5, 2006. President Gordon B. Hinckley presided and dedicated the site.

Dedicated
March 20 to 22, 2009, by President Thomas S. Monson.

Architecture
FFKR Architects. Temple white granite from China with bronze doors. 58,300 square feet, rising 168 feet 8 inches to the top of the gold leafed angel Moroni statue.

Ordinance Rooms
Four instruction rooms in a two stage progressive arrangement, five sealing rooms, one baptistry.

Construction Story

  • Four and a half years passed between the announcement and the dedication, with construction running from August 2006 into early 2009.
  • The public open house ran January 15 through March 14, 2009, excluding Sundays, and drew 684,721 visitors.

Symbolism & Design

  • The sego lily, Utah's state flower, recurs throughout the building in art glass by Utah artist Tom Holdman and in the decorative painting.
  • The art glass also carries a Log Cabin quilt pattern. The building holds 221 exterior windows, 50 interior windows and 432 door panels, made up of 35,420 hand cut pieces of glass.
  • Makore wood, imported from the west coast of central Africa, is used in the doors, trim, paneling and cabinets. Limestone from France appears elsewhere in the interior.
  • Hand painted panoramas by Utah and Colorado artists show mountain scenes, Draper's Corner Canyon and views across the Salt Lake Valley.
  • The baptistry sits on the downhill western side, with floor to ceiling windows that fill it with daylight.

Trivia

  • One of the sealing rooms seats up to 80 guests.
  • Nearly 685,000 people toured the temple across the two month open house.

Temple facts verified as of August 20, 2026.
