Ogden Utah Temple

Location
Ogden, Utah, on 9.96 acres occupying a full downtown block along Washington Boulevard.

Temple Number
The 14th dedicated temple, and the fifth in Utah.

Announced
August 24, 1967, alongside a companion temple for Provo.

Groundbreaking
September 8, 1969. Hugh B. Brown broke ground; Joseph Fielding Smith and Alvin R. Dyer dedicated the site the same day.

Dedicated
January 18 to 20, 1972, by President Joseph Fielding Smith, in six sessions. It was the first temple he dedicated as President of the Church.

Rededicated
September 21, 2014, by President Thomas S. Monson, in three sessions.

Architecture
Emil B. Fetzer designed the original building; Richardson Design Partnership of Salt Lake City designed the 2010 to 2014 rebuild. Granite quarried and fabricated in China. 112,232 square feet, rising 188 feet 8 inches to the top of the angel Moroni statue.

Ordinance Rooms
Six instruction rooms, nine sealing rooms, one baptistry.

Construction Story

  • The original open house ran December 16 through 30, 1971 and drew more than 150,000 visitors.
  • The Church announced the remodel on February 17, 2010. The temple closed April 2, 2011, nearly forty years after it opened, and stayed closed about three and a half years.
  • The second open house ran August 1 through September 6, 2014 and drew roughly 500,000 to 600,000 visitors.
  • The main entrance moved from the west side of the building to the east, facing Washington Boulevard. The project also added underground parking and a water feature.

Symbolism & Design

  • Interior motifs run to the Pioneer Rose, grass blades, a woven pattern and a sunburst.
  • The stonework uses Egyptian marbles: Sunny Vinato, Tiberias Gold and Gallala.
  • Art glass was produced in Indiana and West Virginia. Woodwork is African sapele and painted American maple.

Changes Over Time

  • The 2010 to 2014 project reshaped the exterior completely rather than restoring it. The Church noted at the time that the new design was not remotely similar to the original.
  • Sealing rooms went from eleven in the original building to nine.
  • A main level mural by Robert Shepherd was kept through the rebuild. Keith Bond painted new murals for the baptistry.

Trivia

  • It was the first temple built with six ordinance rooms, an arrangement that let a session begin every twenty minutes.
  • It was the first temple dedicated in the state of Utah, as distinct from the earlier four dedicated in Utah Territory.
  • Its district reaches from Brigham City south to Syracuse and east into Wyoming.

Temple facts verified as of August 23, 2026.
