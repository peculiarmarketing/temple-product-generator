Boise Idaho Temple

Location
Boise, Idaho. The temple stands at 1211 South Cole Road on a 4.83-acre site beside Interstate 84 at the Cole Road exit, at an elevation of 2,754 feet, where its white granite and six spires make it a landmark for freeway travelers.

Temple Number
The 27th dedicated temple of the Church, and the second built in Idaho, following the Idaho Falls Idaho Temple (1945).

Announced
March 31, 1982, by President Gordon B. Hinckley, then a counselor in the First Presidency. Ground was broken before the year was out.

Groundbreaking
December 18, 1982. Elder Mark E. Petersen of the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles presided and dedicated the site; about 5,000 members gathered on the site in the December cold to watch.

Dedicated
May 25 through 30, 1984, by President Gordon B. Hinckley, then second counselor in the First Presidency, in 24 sessions. About 29,000 people attended.

Rededicated
Twice: February 14, 1987, by Elder James E. Faust, after an addition; and November 18, 2012, by President Thomas S. Monson, in three sessions, after a 15-month renovation.

Architecture
Designed by the Church architectural staff with Ron Thurber & Associates of Boise, a modern adaptation of the six-spire design. The exterior is temple white granite quarried in China, beneath a broad sloping roof and six detached spires. The temple encloses 35,868 square feet and rises 112 feet to the angel Moroni statue on the east tower.

Ordinance Rooms
Four instruction rooms, three sealing rooms, and a baptistry.

First Temple President
Seth D. Redford, 1984 to 1987.

Construction Story

  • In 1939, a group of prominent Boise businessmen offered President Heber J. Grant any available site in the city, hoping Idaho's first temple would rise in the capital. Idaho Falls was chosen instead, and President Grant told the group that a temple would come to Boise when membership there increased.
  • Organizers expected about 70,000 visitors at the public open house, held May 1 through 19, 1984. They got 128,716.
  • Convert baptisms in the area more than doubled in the month after the open house, and people called the mission office asking how they could be baptized.
  • The 24 dedicatory sessions were, at the time, the most held for any temple since the Salt Lake Temple in 1893.

Symbolism & Design

  • The six detached spires made Boise the first six-spire temple built since the Washington D.C. Temple in 1974, and before that the Salt Lake Temple in 1893.
  • Syringa, Idaho's state flower, appears throughout the interior in art glass and decorative painting, set against blues, greens, and earth tones. The art glass is the work of Holdman Studios, with murals by Dale Jolley.
  • Glass from the temple's original windows was crushed and worked into the new art glass installed during the 2012 renovation, so the first building's windows survive inside the current ones.

Changes Over Time

  • Heavy use forced an expansion just two and a half years after dedication. Elder James E. Faust rededicated the enlarged temple on February 14, 1987; the addition brought a new baptistry, additional offices, and expanded patron facilities.
  • The temple closed on July 11, 2011, for a 15-month renovation led by CTA Architects Engineers of Boise, with structural reinforcement, an expanded basement, and new grounds featuring a stone-encased reflecting pool. The original marble tile exterior was replaced with temple white granite after the adhesive behind the tile began to seep through and discolor the walls.
  • A new gold-leafed angel Moroni was set atop the east spire on February 16, 2012, and the cornerstone, which had been inside the building since the 1987 expansion, was moved to the exterior northeast corner on August 29, 2012.
  • Nearly 170,000 people toured the renovated temple during the fall 2012 open house. The night before the rededication, 9,200 youth performed a cultural celebration titled "Treasure the Light," with 4,000 dancers, 99 fiddlers, and a choir of more than 1,000 voices.

Trivia

  • Boise is a sister building to the Dallas Texas and Chicago Illinois temples, and its efficiency-driven design was adapted for 14 other temples built between 1984 and 1989.
  • Before the 1984 open house, Idaho Governor John Evans, Lieutenant Governor David Leroy, and Secretary of State Pete Cenarusa toured the temple in a special preview, along with 246 ministers of other faiths and their family members.
  • At its 2012 rededication, the temple served about 100,000 members in 31 stakes across western Idaho and eastern Oregon.

Temple facts verified as of August 25, 2026.
