Smithfield Utah Temple

Status
Under construction, with major construction complete and crews finishing the interior; furnishings began arriving in spring 2026. Dates for the public open house and dedication have not yet been announced. Michael L. Staheli and Linda A. Staheli of the Smithfield Utah North Stake were called in March 2026 as the temple's first president and matron.

Location
Smithfield, Utah. The temple stands on a 13.3-acre site at 800 West and 100 North in northern Cache Valley, at an elevation of 4,506 feet, roughly seven miles from the Logan Utah Temple.

Temple Number
The second temple in Cache County, following the Logan Utah Temple, dedicated in 1884. Its number among the Church's dedicated temples will be fixed at its dedication.

Announced
April 4, 2021, by President Russell M. Nelson during the 191st Annual General Conference, one of 20 temples announced that day.

Groundbreaking
June 18, 2022. Elder Quentin L. Cook of the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles presided and dedicated the site, with Elder Gary E. Stevenson conducting. About 600 people attended in person, and the ceremony was broadcast to the 13 stakes of the temple district.

Architecture
A three-story temple of approximately 81,000 square feet. The exterior features columns of arched windows and arched entryways, with a tall domed cupola near the front of the building and a smaller dome at the opposite end.

Ordinance Rooms
Four instruction rooms, four sealing rooms, and two baptistries.

Construction Story

  • Ground was broken by two Apostles who grew up in Cache Valley. Elder Cook and Elder Stevenson are both Utah State University graduates, and Elder Cook told the gathering that the valley "always feels like home to me. It is among the most beautiful places on earth."
  • In the site dedicatory prayer, Elder Cook prayed that the temple would become "a beacon light for the communities it serves" and gave thanks "for those who have gone before us."
  • The temple rises on ground the LaMont Poulsen family farmed for more than 160 years before it became a temple site.
  • The main dome was set in June 2024 and the rear cupola that August; exterior stonework was substantially complete by the end of 2024.

Symbolism & Design

  • The arch is the building's repeating motif: columns of arched windows wrap the exterior and arched entryways frame the entrance, beneath the temple's two domes.

Trivia

  • Cache County has the second-highest concentration of Latter-day Saints of any Utah county, at about 64 percent of residents.

Temple facts verified as of August 19, 2026.
