Layton Utah Temple

Location
Layton, Utah, in Davis County, on the bench between the Wasatch Range and the Great Salt Lake.

Temple Number
The 195th dedicated temple.

Announced
April 1, 2018, by President Russell M. Nelson, in the Sunday afternoon session of general conference.

Groundbreaking
May 23, 2020. Elder Craig C. Christensen, president of the Utah Area, presided, and Elder Randy D. Funk offered the site dedicatory prayer.

Dedicated
June 16, 2024, by Elder David A. Bednar of the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles, in two sessions.

Architecture
HNWL Architects of Salt Lake City, with landscape architecture by MGB+A. Precast concrete panels over a structural steel frame and cast in place concrete shear walls. 93,539 square feet, rising 215 feet, with two attached end spires and an angel Moroni statue.

Ordinance Rooms
Four instruction rooms, six sealing rooms, one baptistry.

Construction Story

  • The Church acquired the property on March 30, 2018, two days before the temple was announced.
  • The groundbreaking was held a week early and without advance public notice because of COVID-19 restrictions. A video of the scaled down ceremony was released on May 30, 2020, the date the public event had been scheduled for.
  • The public open house ran April 19 through June 1, 2024, excluding Sundays.

Symbolism & Design

  • The building is styled after Art Nouveau and draws its motifs from Davis County's farming history, when the area was known as the Garden Spot of Utah. Abstracted fruit trees, blossoms, seedlings and leafy branches run through the ornament.
  • The art glass follows that idea in sequence, showing stylized seedlings growing into fruit trees in deep saturated color.
  • A restored Tiffany Studios window titled "The Resurrection," made around 1915, stands at the entrance to the second floor patron waiting area. It was bought from a United Presbyterian church in Amenia, New York, before that building was demolished in 2015.
  • Seven original works of art were commissioned for the building.

Trivia

  • The Tiffany window is roughly a century older than the building it now hangs in.
  • Layton was dedicated on the same day as the Salta Argentina Temple.

Temple facts verified as of August 20, 2026.
