Oakland California Temple

Location
Oakland, California. The temple stands on an 18.1 acre hillside site at 4770 Lincoln Avenue in the Oakland hills, with commanding views west across San Francisco Bay.

Temple Number
The 13th dedicated temple, and the second built in California.

Announced
January 23, 1961, by President David O. McKay.

Groundbreaking
May 26, 1962. President David O. McKay presided, joined by his counselors in the First Presidency.

Dedicated
November 17 to 19, 1964, by President David O. McKay in six sessions. More than 120 general authorities and their spouses attended.

Rededicated
June 16, 2019, by President Dallin H. Oaks in three sessions, accompanied by Elder David A. Bednar.

Architecture
Designed by Harold W. Burton, who spent a career designing buildings for the Church and worked the idiom of the early 1960s into this one. Reinforced concrete faced with Sierra white granite quarried at Raymond, California. About 95,000 square feet, with five spires: a central tower reaching 170 feet surrounded by four smaller ones.

Ordinance Rooms
Four instruction rooms, seven sealing rooms, and one baptistry.

Construction Story

  • The building was completed on September 27, 1964, a little over two years after ground was broken.
  • The public open house ran October 5 through October 31, 1964, and was extended an additional week. About 347,000 people toured the temple.
  • Its dedication made California the second state in the country with more than one temple.
  • The 2018 renovation drew materials from across the world: flooring stone from Greece, China, and Georgia, primavera wood from South America for the celestial room doors, ribbon striped mahogany from Africa in some sealing rooms, and custom bronze hardware made from Indian brass.

Symbolism & Design

  • The five spire arrangement is the building's signature, and it is a deliberate borrowing. Burton's design points toward sacred architecture of the Far East, the Taj Mahal and Angkor Wat among them, chosen to reflect the range of people living in Northern California.
  • Two granite relief panels, each about 35 feet by 13 feet, carry the building's narrative. The north panel depicts Christ teaching, drawn from Matthew 5 and 6. The south panel depicts the resurrected Savior appearing to the Nephites, from 3 Nephi 11.
  • A cascading waterfall and creek style water feature runs through the gardens at the front of the temple.

Changes Over Time

  • The original waterfall was removed in 1969 because the leaks could not be repaired. Modern materials made it possible to restore it during the renovation fifty years later.
  • The temple closed in February 2018 for a renovation of about 16 months, followed by a public open house May 11 through June 1, 2019.

Trivia

  • It was the first temple in the United States designed for a film presentation of the endowment, and the first with multiple instruction rooms feeding into a central celestial room. Nearly every temple built since has followed that plan.
  • The front doors malfunctioned shortly after the 1964 dedication, and patrons used a side entrance for years afterward.
  • The interstake center on the grounds, built in 1959, once served as the practice facility for the Golden State Warriors.
