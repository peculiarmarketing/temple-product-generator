Brigham City Utah Temple

Location
Brigham City, Utah. A 3.1 acre site on downtown Main Street, directly across the street from the historic Box Elder Stake Tabernacle.

Temple Number
The 139th dedicated temple of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, the 14th in Utah, and the first in Box Elder County.

Announced
October 3, 2009, by President Thomas S. Monson at the 179th Semiannual General Conference, one of five temples announced that morning.

Groundbreaking
July 31, 2010. President Boyd K. Packer, president of the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles and a Brigham City native, presided.

Dedicated
September 23, 2012, by President Boyd K. Packer, in three sessions.

Architecture
FFKR Architects, with Big-D Construction as general contractor. The design deliberately echoes Utah's pioneer-era temples. Warm white precast concrete exterior, 36,000 square feet, twin end spires and an angel Moroni statue, rising 165 feet.

Ordinance Rooms
Two instruction rooms in a two-stage progressive arrangement, three sealing rooms, and a baptistry whose font rests on twelve cast bronze oxen.

First Temple President
Preston J. Checketts, with Louise B. Checketts as matron, 2012 to 2015.

Construction Story

  • The temple stands on the site of the old Central School, a three-story elementary school built in 1901 and rebuilt after a 1947 fire. Boyd K. Packer attended grade school there as a boy; six decades later he returned to break ground for a temple on the same spot, then dedicated it in his hometown.
  • The angel Moroni statue was set in place on July 12, 2011, more than a year before the building was finished.
  • The public open house ran from August 18 to September 15, 2012, and drew nearly 404,500 visitors. The busiest single day was Labor Day, when 25,000 people walked through.

Symbolism & Design

  • Peach blossoms run through the whole building: in the art glass of the second-floor windows, in the furniture, and in the hand-carved carpet of the celestial room. Elder William R. Walker explained during the 2012 open house that the motif carries no religious significance; it is a tribute to the area's fruit-growing heritage.
  • Twenty-six fruit trees were planted on the temple grounds, continuing the same nod to the region's orchards.
  • The exterior deliberately recalls the Church's pioneer-era temples such as Salt Lake and Manti. Elder Walker put it simply: "It looks like a historic temple."
  • The sealing waiting room displays portraits of Brigham Young, the city's namesake, and Lorenzo Snow, who served as stake president there.

Trivia

  • Brigham City was one of five temples announced in a single conference session, alongside Concepción Chile, Fortaleza Brazil, Fort Lauderdale Florida, and Sapporo Japan.
  • In that announcement, President Monson noted that 83 percent of Church members lived within 200 miles of a temple.
  • At its dedication the temple served about 40,000 Latter-day Saints in 13 stakes across northern Utah and southeastern Idaho.
