San Antonio Texas Temple

Location
San Antonio, Texas. The temple sits on about 5.5 acres atop a prominent hill in the Stone Oak area of north San Antonio, at an elevation of 1,225 feet.

Temple Number
The Church's 120th operating temple at its dedication.

Announced
June 24, 2001. The site was announced August 19, 2002.

Groundbreaking
March 29, 2003. Elder H. Bruce Stucki of the Seventy presided and dedicated the site.

Dedicated
May 22, 2005, by President Gordon B. Hinckley, in four sessions attended by 14,069 members.

Architecture
Rehler, Vaughn & Koone, architects; Jacobsen Construction, contractor. Traditional single-spire design with a granite exterior, 97 feet wide by 191 feet long, rising 115 feet to the top of the angel Moroni statue, with approximately 16,800 square feet of floor area.

Ordinance Rooms
Two instruction rooms in a two-stage progressive design, two sealing rooms, and a baptistry with the font resting on twelve oxen.

First Temple President
A. Ray Otte, called as the temple's first president for its 2005 opening.

Construction Story

  • Local members did not just watch the temple rise. Members of one Spanish-speaking branch came to the site and washed construction bricks, and members across the area hand-delivered 16-page open house brochures through their neighborhoods.
  • The 13-foot gold-leafed angel Moroni statue was placed atop the spire on September 21, 2004, eight months before the dedication.
  • The night before the dedication, local youth performed the Heart of Texas Youth Jubilee, a cultural celebration held at the Alamodome.

Symbolism & Design

  • The temple stands on one of the highest points in the area, and its lighted spire can be seen from roughly 30 miles away.
  • Every window carries art glass, and the grounds feature water features and landscaping chosen to reflect the rugged beauty of south Texas.
  • At the dedication, Elder Charles Didier called the hilltop setting deliberate, noting that temples built on elevated ground prompt elevated thoughts.

Trivia

  • It was the fourth temple built in Texas, following Dallas, Houston, and Lubbock.
  • The three-week open house drew 69,679 visitors between April 16 and May 7, 2005, with members organizing chartered buses from a local high school to handle the crowds.
  • At its dedication the temple served eleven stakes and two districts across southwest Texas.

Temple facts verified as of August 17, 2026.
