Rome Italy Temple

Location
Rome, Italy. The temple stands on an elevated 15 acre site on Via di Settebagni in northeast Rome, beside the Grande Raccordo Anulare that rings the city. The grounds also hold a visitors' center, a meetinghouse, a family history center, and patron housing.

Temple Number
The 162nd dedicated temple, the first in Italy, and the thirteenth in Europe.

Announced
October 4, 2008, by President Thomas S. Monson during general conference.

Groundbreaking
October 23, 2010. President Thomas S. Monson presided.

Dedicated
March 10 to 12, 2019, by President Russell M. Nelson in seven sessions. President Nelson conducted the cornerstone ceremony on the first day.

Architecture
Designed by Niels Valentiner. A Bianco Sardo granite facade over cast in place concrete, three stories and about 40,000 square feet, standing 156 feet 3 inches with two attached end spires and an angel Moroni statue. Interiors are finished in Perlato Svevo and Cenia marble, sapele, burl and cherry wood, with light fixtures of Murano glass from Venice.

Ordinance Rooms
Two instruction rooms, three sealing rooms, and one baptistry.

Construction Story

  • Eight and a half years passed between the groundbreaking in 2010 and the dedication in 2019, one of the longer builds of the modern era.
  • The temple was built on a former farm, and olive trees between 400 and 500 years old already stood on the ground when the site was chosen.
  • The public open house ran January 14 through February 16, 2019. About 52,000 people toured the temple. Elder David A. Bednar and Elder Ronald A. Rasband led the first tours for journalists.
  • The angel Moroni statue was installed on March 25, 2017, two years before the dedication.

Symbolism & Design

  • The oval piazza and the curves of the building take their cue from Roman architecture, and specifically from Borromini's church of San Carlo alle Quattro Fontane, known in the city as San Carlino.
  • Floor patterns inside the temple are drawn from Michelangelo's paving at the Piazza del Campidoglio.
  • A floor to ceiling art glass wall at the entrance depicts a scene from the life of Christ. Other windows take their forms from the olive trees on the site, which carry their own association with the Resurrection.

Trivia

  • On March 11, 2019, the entire First Presidency and Quorum of the Twelve Apostles gathered in the temple's visitors' center. It was the first time the two bodies had assembled together anywhere outside the United States.
  • The visitors' center holds digitally replicated statues of Jesus Christ and the Twelve Apostles, based on the originals Bertel Thorvaldsen carved in the 1800s, along with a fountain representing living water.
  • The dedication came nearly 170 years after Lorenzo Snow began missionary work in Italy in 1850. Writing of that beginning, Snow said he had "opened a door which no man can shut." He also recorded, in an account published in 1851, that "the work here is slow and tedious. Nevertheless, the Church has been established. The tree has been planted and is spreading its roots."
  • The day before the dedication began, on March 9, 2019, President Nelson met with Pope Francis at the Vatican, the first formal audience between a president of the Church and a pope.
