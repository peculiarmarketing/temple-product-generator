Cedar City Utah Temple

Location
Cedar City, Utah, in the red rock country of the state's southwest corner.

Temple Number
The 159th dedicated temple, and the 17th in Utah.

Announced
April 6, 2013, by President Thomas S. Monson, in the Saturday morning session of general conference.

Groundbreaking
August 8, 2015. Elder L. Whitney Clayton of the Presidency of the Seventy presided.

Dedicated
December 10, 2017, by President Henry B. Eyring, First Counselor in the First Presidency.

Architecture
Architectural Nexus. Beige precast concrete panels with sections of gypsum fiber reinforced concrete. 39,802 square feet, rising 160 feet 6 inches to a single attached end tower carrying an angel Moroni statue.

Ordinance Rooms
Two instruction rooms, three sealing rooms, one baptistry.

Construction Story

  • Four and a half years passed between the announcement and the dedication, with construction running from August 2015 to late 2017.
  • The public open house ran October 27 through November 18, 2017.

Symbolism & Design

  • The building was designed to resemble a pioneer-era temple, using colors and textures common to that period.
  • Two stained glass windows depicting the Savior were donated by a Latter-day Saint art collector.
  • Eight paintings by local artists show regional landscapes, including Bryce and Kolob canyons. Local flowers and the surrounding red cliffs recur in the rugs, woodwork and paintings throughout.
  • Local flora also appears in the art glass and millwork, alongside traditional Sheraton style elements.

Trivia

  • About 187,000 people toured the temple during the three week open house.
  • The angel Moroni statue was set in place on September 15, 2016, drawing a large crowd of onlookers.

Temple facts verified as of August 20, 2026.
