Ogden Utah Temple, Original Design (1972–2011)

Status
This drawing shows the temple as it stood from its 1972 dedication until it closed for renovation on April 2, 2011. The building still stands and still operates, but the 2010 to 2014 project replaced the exterior entirely. The Church noted at the time that the new design was not remotely similar to the original, so the building described here is no longer the building you can visit.

Location
Ogden, Utah, on 9.96 acres occupying a full downtown block along Washington Boulevard.

Temple Number
The 14th dedicated temple, and the fifth in Utah.

Announced
August 24, 1967, alongside a companion temple for Provo.

Groundbreaking
September 8, 1969. Hugh B. Brown broke ground; Joseph Fielding Smith and Alvin R. Dyer dedicated the site the same day.

Dedicated
January 18 to 20, 1972, by President Joseph Fielding Smith, in six sessions. It was the first temple he dedicated as President of the Church.

Architecture
Church architect Emil B. Fetzer. White exterior panels rising to a single central spire, roughly 115,000 square feet.

Ordinance Rooms
Six instruction rooms, eleven sealing rooms, one baptistry.

Construction Story

  • Fetzer designed Ogden and Provo to one basic plan. Building the two temples from a shared design was a deliberate economy, and it is why photographs of the two buildings from these years are so easily confused.
  • The open house ran December 16 through 30, 1971 and drew more than 150,000 visitors.
  • Just over two years passed between the September 1969 groundbreaking and the December 1971 open house.

Symbolism & Design

  • The six ordinance rooms were the point of the design. Arranging them around a central core let a session begin every twenty minutes, which no earlier temple could do.
  • The original plan called for an angel Moroni statue on the gold colored spire. It was cut from the design before construction.

Changes Over Time

  • An angel Moroni statue was finally added more than thirty years after the dedication.
  • The Church announced the remodel on February 17, 2010. The temple closed in April 2011 and was rededicated by President Thomas S. Monson on September 21, 2014 with a completely new exterior, a main entrance moved from west to east, underground parking and a water feature.
  • The rebuilt temple is smaller than the original, 112,232 square feet against roughly 115,000, and has nine sealing rooms rather than eleven.

Trivia

  • It was the first temple built with six ordinance rooms.
  • It was the first temple dedicated in the state of Utah, as distinct from the earlier four dedicated in Utah Territory.
  • Its twin in Provo kept the shared design for another decade, until a redesign of that temple was announced in 2021.

Temple facts verified as of August 23, 2026.
